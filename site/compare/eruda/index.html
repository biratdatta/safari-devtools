Safari DevTools vs Eruda

Updated 2026-10-20 · 2 min read

Eruda is a popular open-source console for mobile browsers. Safari DevTools works the same way, by running inside the page, but aims to feel like Chrome DevTools and goes further on debugging and testing.

Where Eruda shines

Eruda is small, battle-tested and has a plugin system, with plugins for things like timing, fps and DOM features. If you only need a quick console on a phone and like its mobile layout, it does the job well.

Where Safari DevTools goes further

  • Errors point to your real code. Stack traces are mapped through source maps to your TypeScript, with original function names.
  • Test the unhappy paths. Mock, block or delay any request, or throttle to 3G or offline, without a proxy.
  • Check accessibility with a screen-reader tree, a numbered tab order on the page and contrast fixes from your tokens.
  • Familiar if you know Chrome. The same tabs, shortcuts and look as Chrome DevTools.
  • Fits your workflow: extensions for Safari and Chrome, remote mode to watch an iPhone from your Mac, and an API for Playwright and AI agents.

Switching from Eruda

Replace the Eruda script and its eruda.init() call with one line, placed first in <head>:

<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/biratdatta/safari-devtools@v1.5.2/standalone/safari-devtools.js"></script>

No init call is needed. A round button appears in the corner; tap it, or press Ctrl ⇧ D on a keyboard.
